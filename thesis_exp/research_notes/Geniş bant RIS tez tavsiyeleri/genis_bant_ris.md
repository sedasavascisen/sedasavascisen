## Q1. Which papers design a single RIS phase vector for multi-carrier/OFDM systems, with which objectives, algorithms, complexity and convergence?

### Takeaway
The standard formulation, from 2019 onward, uses one reflection vector shared by all subcarriers. The outer loop is alternating optimization or BCD. Objectives are the sum of per-subcarrier rates for one user, or the average sum rate over subcarriers for MU-MISO (via WMMSE/MSE equivalence). Max-min variants appeared from 2023 and use gradient or SCA methods. Most papers report complexity in terms of N (RIS elements). Reported per-iteration cost grows linearly in the number of subcarriers K, because the objective and gradient are sums over subcarriers. However, I found no paper that treats K as the knob to turn.

### Cited Findings
- **Yang, Zheng, Zhang, Zhang, "Intelligent Reflecting Surface Meets OFDM: Protocol Design and Rate Maximization", IEEE TCOM, July 2020.** DOI 10.1109/TCOMM.2020.2981458; arXiv 1906.09956. Single user, frequency-selective channel. It jointly optimizes BS power allocation and a common IRS reflection vector, alternating between the two, with a customized initialization. Adjacent elements are grouped to share one coefficient, which cuts training overhead. There is an optimal group size that trades training overhead against beamforming flexibility (abstract). — [arXiv:1906.09956](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.09956), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TCOMM.2020.2981458)
- **Yang, Zhang, Zhang, "IRS-Enhanced OFDM: Power Allocation and Passive Array Optimization", GLOBECOM 2019.** DOI 10.1109/GLOBECOM38437.2019.9014204; arXiv 1905.00604. This is the conference precursor: rate maximization by alternating power allocation and passive array optimization (abstract). — [arXiv:1905.00604](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00604)
- **Zheng & Zhang, "Intelligent Reflecting Surface-Enhanced OFDM: Channel Estimation and Reflection Optimization", IEEE WCL, 2020** (the arXiv journal-ref says 2019; Crossref says April 2020). DOI 10.1109/LWC.2019.2961357; arXiv 1909.03272. The reflection is optimized by a low-complexity algorithm "based on the resolved strongest signal path in the time domain" (abstract). The fundamental limitation is that "the same configuration must be used over the entire bandwidth". ### Inferences
- When the objective is the sum over k of log det(·) or the sum over k of rates, every gradient, manifold or BCD step that touches the phase vector costs O(K·N·M·U) or more. Here K is the number of subcarriers, N the RIS elements, M the BS antennas, and U the users. This holds as long as the per-subcarrier precoder is recomputed (ZF/MMSE per subcarrier is O(K·M·U²) or more). This is an inference from the problem structure; none of the abstracts above states it as a scaling law in K.
- Deep unfolding applied specifically to wideband single-phase RIS appears thin. My arXiv query "wideband AND RIS AND deep unfolding" returned nothing. The Kaplan survey also does not mention deep unfolding in the 100k characters I read.

## Q2. Which papers quantify beam squint / spatial-wideband effects for RIS, and under which conditions does the loss become significant?

### Takeaway
Beam squint for RIS is studied mostly at mmWave/THz with large apertures (XL-RIS, near field). Frequency-flat phase control loses array gain because the phase needed per element depends on frequency, and the mismatch grows with aperture × fractional bandwidth. The abstracts give no closed-form "significance threshold". The most explicit treatment is a 2026 "first-null bandwidth" approximation for the aperture-bandwidth limit of frequency-flat RIS control.

### Cited Findings
- **Chen, Chen, Jiang, "Beam-Squint Mitigating for Reconfigurable Intelligent Surface Aided Wideband MmWave Communications", WCNC 2021.** DOI 10.1109/WCNC49053.2021.9417413; arXiv 2103.11105. It states the core contradiction: RIS phases "should be the same for all frequencies", but beam squint needs different phases per frequency. This matters when "the RIS usually consists of enormous elements and the bandwidth … may be up to several GHz". For LoS, the common phase is obtained by maximizing an upper bound on achievable rate. For NLoS, a mean channel covariance matrix (MCCM) scheme exploits correlation across paths and subcarriers (abstract). — [arXiv:2103.11105](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.11105)
- **Dovelos, Assimonis, Ngo, Bellalta, Matthaiou, "Intelligent Reflecting Surface-Aided Wideband THz Communications: Modeling and Analysis", arXiv 2110.15768.** It models the near field and the spatial-wideband effect, shows that "conventional frequency-flat beamfocusing significantly reduces the power gain due to beam squint", and characterizes the loss analytically for holographic surfaces (abstract). Journal DOI: UNVERIFIED (Crossref rate-limited). — [arXiv:2110.15768](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.15768)
- **Hao, You, Zhou, Chu, Sun, Xiao, "The Far-/Near-Field Beam Squint and Solutions for THz Intelligent Reflecting Surface Communications", IEEE TVT, Aug 2023.** DOI 10.1109/TVT.2023.3254153; arXiv 2208.12385. It analyzes beam-gain loss from far- and near-field squint in the cascaded BS–IRS–user channel. The proposed fix is a DAM (see Q4) (abstract). — [arXiv:2208.12385](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.12385)
- **Ma, Shen, An et al., "Wideband Channel Estimation for IRS-Aided Systems in the Face of Beam Squint", IEEE TWC, Oct 2021.** DOI 10.1109/TWC.2021.3072694; arXiv 2106.02883. Beam squint on the estimation side. — [arXiv:2106.02883](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02883)
- **Khalid, Kwong, Chieng, Liu, "Beam Squint and Aperture–Bandwidth Limitations in Wideband RIS-Assisted THz Links", arXiv 2609.14037 (Sept 2026; accepted to IoTCCT 2026, a minor venue).** It derives the ergodic rate under squint plus phase quantization, and "a first-null bandwidth approximation characterizes the aperture–bandwidth limitation of frequency-flat RIS control". The formula is not in the abstract. — [arXiv:2609.14037](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14037)
- **Saeidi & Tabassum, "Characterization of Beam-Squint and Beam-Split Effects in RIS-assisted Multi-Frequency Networks", arXiv 2609.07995 (Sept 2026).** It gives closed-form peak conditions for a UPA RIS configured at one frequency and illuminated at another, as functions of frequency ratio, angles and element spacing, including b-bit quantization harmonics (abstract). — [arXiv:2609.07995](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07995)
- **Qian, Hu, Liu, Peng, "Wideband Beamforming with RIS: A Unified Framework via Space-Frequency Transformation", arXiv 2407.05293 (2024).** It uses a space-frequency Fourier transform plus the stationary-phase method to get an approximate closed-form RIS phase that produces a flat in-band beampattern. It "significantly reduces the signal processing complexity, compared to the existing approaches" (abstract). — [arXiv:2407.05293](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.05293)
- **Yu & Dai, "Near-Field Wideband Beamforming for RIS Based on Fresnel Zones", IEEE TCOM, July 2025** (DOI 10.1109/TCOMM.2024.3523960); conference version GLOBECOM 2024, DOI 10.1109/GLOBECOM52923.2024.10900936; arXiv 2411.18878. Phases are aligned along Fresnel zones, where beam split vanishes. Across zones the design uses stationary phase plus Gerchberg-Saxton, and it proves that a uniform in-band gain gives an upper bound on rate (abstract). — [arXiv:2411.18878](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18878)
- **"Achievable Rate Optimization of the RIS-Aided Near-Field Wideband Uplink", IEEE TWC, March 2024.** DOI 10.1109/TWC.2023.3297396. A virtual-subarray phase design against squint, MU uplink. A search snippet says sum rate "can be doubled" versus benchmarks: UNVERIFIED (not read). — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TWC.2023.3297396)
- **Zhu, Wu, Chen, Liu, Liu, "A Flexible Design for Beam Squint Effect Suppression in IRS-Aided THz Communications", arXiv 2508.21295 (2025).** Movable antennas at the BS plus movable IRS subarrays. It maximizes the minimum received power across the band by MM (abstract). — [arXiv:2508.21295](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21295)

### Inferences
- A standard array-theory inference, not taken from the abstracts above: for a ULA-like RIS of N elements at half-wavelength spacing, the frequency-flat phase loses noticeable gain when the band-edge phase error across the aperture approaches roughly π. This happens when N · (B/f_c) · |sin θ| reaches order 1 or more (roughly, the fractional bandwidth times N is about 1 or more). With N around 64 per dimension and B/f_c of a few percent (e.g. 100 MHz at 3.5 GHz, about 2.9%), N·B/f_c is about 1.8. Squint is then borderline at oblique angles and negligible near broadside. With N of 8–16 and sub-6 GHz, it is negligible. **The thesis should compute this for its own geometry and not cite a threshold. I found no peer-reviewed closed-form threshold in a verified abstract.**
- In typical sub-6 GHz OFDM RIS setups with modest N, the dominant wideband effect on a single phase vector is multipath frequency selectivity (different taps or delays per element), not array squint. This matches the Yang/Zheng/Li framing, where the channel is frequency-selective and no squint is modeled.

### Gaps
- The first-null bandwidth formula in 2609.14037 and the analytical loss expression in 2110.15768 were not extracted, because the full texts were not read.
- I found no paper that reports loss versus fractional bandwidth for mid-band (3–7 GHz) MU-MISO RIS.

## Q3. Are there works using subcarrier subsampling, frequency-domain compression, or "representative subcarrier" approximations for RIS phase design?

### Takeaway
**I found no paper that explicitly optimizes the RIS phase on a subset of K_opt subcarriers and evaluates it on the whole band, nor one that sweeps K_opt against accuracy and runtime.** That was true across arXiv API queries (subset, subsampling, representative) and web searches. The Kaplan 2026 survey of 78 works does not mention subcarrier subsets, subband grouping or frequency compression in the portion I read, and it flags frequency selectivity as open. Related compression strategies do exist:
- time-domain dominant-tap approximations (Zheng & Zhang; An et al. DAM);
- covariance averaging across subcarriers (MCCM, Chen et al.);
- codebook/training-set search with complexity independent of K (An, Wu, Yuen);
- closed-form flat-beampattern designs (Qian et al.; Yu & Dai).

This gap supports the novelty of a K_opt experiment, though it is an absence of evidence from roughly 16 searches, not proof.

### Cited Findings
- **Time-domain strongest-path compression.** Zheng & Zhang optimize the reflection "based on the resolved strongest signal path in the time domain", which replaces the full set of K subcarriers with one dominant tap (abstract). — [arXiv:1909.03272](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03272)
- **Strongest-tap alignment in DAM.** An et al. propose a low-complexity scheme that aligns "the strongest taps of all reflected channels" while keeping the maximum delay spread within the CP. They present it as a way "to circumvent the high complexity for optimizing the RIS reflection coefficients" (abstract). — [arXiv:2110.09291](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.09291)
- **Mean channel covariance across subcarriers.** Chen et al. build a mean channel covariance matrix that exploits correlation "between both the paths and the subcarriers" to obtain one common phase (abstract). This compresses K subcarriers into one N×N matrix. — [arXiv:2103.11105](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.11105)
- **Codebook search that sidesteps per-K optimization.** An, Wu, Yuen, "Scalable Channel Estimation and Reflection Optimization for RIS-Enhanced OFDM Systems", IEEE WCL, April 2022, DOI 10.1109/LWC.2022.3145885; arXiv 2110.12799. A training set of reflection vectors is generated offline. Only the composite channel is estimated for each, and the best vector is picked. Training overhead can be "flexibly adjust[ed] … according to the given channel coherence time", and the paper analyzes the rate scaling law versus number of training slots (abstract). This is the nearest published analogue of a fidelity-versus-time knob, but the knob is training slots, not subcarriers. — [arXiv:2110.12799](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.12799)
- **Element grouping (a spatial, not spectral, reduction).** Yang et al. find an optimal grouping size under a training-overhead versus flexibility trade-off (abstract). This is a useful methodological parallel: an "optimal reduction level" curve. — [arXiv:1906.09956](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.09956)
- **Survey confirmation of the gap.** Kaplan (2026) says the RIS is mostly treated as frequency-flat with one phase set. It notes that at M = 256–1024 subcarriers a phase tuned for one subcarrier is not good for another, and it lists no subset or subband method (WebFetch of the first 100k of 170k characters). — [arXiv:2606.31334](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31334)

### Inferences
- In the classical OFDM literature, pilot subcarriers or "comb" sampling plus interpolation is the established way to reduce frequency-domain dimension. For an RIS objective, the channel across the band has only L_taps degrees of freedom per element, where L_taps is the number of significant delay taps. Sampling at least L_taps subcarriers, spread uniformly, therefore captures the frequency response in principle (Nyquist in delay). This predicts that the gain from K_opt saturates once K_opt reaches about L_taps (or the delay spread divided by the sampling period). This testable hypothesis is my inference, not a cited result.
- Uniformly spread subcarriers should beat contiguous ones at equal K_opt, for the same reason.

### Gaps
- No accuracy-versus-complexity numbers exist in the literature for subcarrier subsampling in RIS phase design, so there is nothing to compare the thesis result against.
- I did not search IEEE Xplore full text directly (no access). A subset method may exist inside a paper whose abstract does not mention it.

## Q4. Delay-adjustable / TTD RIS designs: what do they solve and at what hardware cost?

### Takeaway
These designs add a per-element (or per-subarray) controllable delay to the phase. Equivalent goals, depending on the paper: make the effective reflection frequency-dependent, cancel squint, align taps within the CP. Element-level delay costs energy, insertion loss and hardware complexity, which is why grouped or sub-surface TTD and virtual-subarray variants exist. No abstract read gives cost numbers.

### Cited Findings
- **An, Xu, Ng, Yuen, Gan, Hanzo, "Reconfigurable Intelligent Surface-Enhanced OFDM Communications via Delay Adjustable Metasurface", arXiv 2110.09291 (2021).** The DAM is varactor-based. Each element stores and retrieves the impinging wave by controlling electromagnetically induced transparency (EIT), which adds an extra delay. The rate is maximized by alternating over power allocation and reflection coefficients plus delays, and a low-complexity strongest-tap alignment is constrained by the CP length (abstract). The journal venue is UNVERIFIED (Crossref was rate-limited; a Southampton eprint exists: [eprints.soton.ac.uk/486785](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/486785/)). — [arXiv:2110.09291](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.09291)
- **Hao et al. (IEEE TVT 2023, DOI 10.1109/TVT.2023.3254153).** It applies the DAM to a THz IRS and jointly optimizes phase shifts and time delays, which "effectively eliminates the beam gain loss caused by beam squint" in both far and near field (abstract). — [arXiv:2208.12385](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.12385)
- **Wang, Xiao, Zou, Xie, Liu, "Wideband Beamforming for RIS Assisted Near-Field Communications", IEEE TWC, Nov 2024.** DOI 10.1109/TWC.2024.3447570; arXiv 2401.11141. It compares a TTD-RIS with a virtual-subarray RIS (SA-RIS) under a deep-learning end-to-end design. The TTD-RIS "can achieve better spectral efficiency than the SA-RIS while requiring additional energy consumption and hardware cost" (abstract). — [arXiv:2401.11141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11141)
- **Sun, Yan, Hao, Huang, Yuen, "Beamforming Design for the Distributed RISs-aided THz Communications with Double-Layer True Time Delays", arXiv 2310.13917.** The double-layer TTD sits at the BS and reduces large-range delay devices, which "involve additional insertion loss and amplification circuitry". Multiple distributed RISs replace one ultra-large RIS to relieve RIS beam split. Finite-resolution phase, delay and reflection are optimized by AO with MMSE plus coordinate updates (abstract). — [arXiv:2310.13917](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13917)
- **"Near-Field Wideband Beamforming for RIS-Assisted THz Communications With FTTDs", IEEE TWC, Nov 2025.** DOI 10.1109/TWC.2025.3570079 (Crossref title match). Authors and content: UNVERIFIED. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TWC.2025.3570079)
- **Element-response alternatives to TTD.** Lorentzian-parameter control (Katsanos et al., arXiv 2202.02169) and selectable phase-frequency profiles (Abbas et al., TWC 2025, arXiv 2308.13660) exploit the natural frequency dispersion of the unit cell rather than adding delay lines (abstracts). — [arXiv:2202.02169](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.02169), [arXiv:2308.13660](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13660)
- **Dispersive time-modulated RIS for wideband OFDM.** Iudice, Darsena, Gelli, arXiv 2609.18360 (Sept 2026): physically consistent modeling. Not read beyond the title: UNVERIFIED content. — [arXiv:2609.18360](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18360)

### Inferences
- For the thesis (a frequency-flat phase vector), DAM/TTD is the "upper-bound hardware" alternative. It is worth one sentence as context, but it changes the variable set (N phases plus N delays), so it is not a direct baseline for the K_opt experiment.

### Gaps
- Quantitative hardware cost (power per TTD element, insertion loss in dB) does not appear in any abstract read.

## Q5. Any wideband RIS work that considers mobility, channel aging, or computation time?

### Takeaway
This intersection is sparse. Lin et al. (JSAC 2020) interleave estimation and beamforming within a frame. An, Wu, Yuen (WCL 2022) tie training overhead to coherence time. Keykhosravi et al. treat mobility plus spatial-wideband effects for localization, not phase design. RIS-OTFS papers handle high mobility, but with a different waveform. The Kaplan survey frames the computation budget as sub-ms against 10–100 μs mmWave coherence times and notes that static optimization must be "re-solv[ed] from scratch" under mobility. **I found no paper that evaluates a wideband single-phase RIS design at measured wall-clock latency on an aging channel.** That is the thesis's niche.

### Cited Findings
- **Lin et al., JSAC 2020.** Progressive CSI estimation and RIS fine-tuning across sub-frames, with reduced delay before data transmission (secondary summary; UNVERIFIED in detail). — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/JSAC.2020.3007038)
- **An, Wu, Yuen, WCL 2022.** Training overhead is adjustable "according to the given channel coherence time" (abstract). — [arXiv:2110.12799](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.12799)
- **Keykhosravi, Keskin, Seco-Granados et al., "RIS-Enabled SISO Localization under User Mobility and Spatial-Wideband Effects", IEEE JSTSP 2022.** DOI 10.1109/JSTSP.2022.3175036; arXiv 2211.09844. Localization, not rate-oriented phase design. — [arXiv:2211.09844](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09844)
- **"RIS-Assisted OTFS Communications: Phase Configuration via Received Energy Maximization", arXiv 2404.07759.** Phase design for high-speed delay-Doppler channels, with more than 4 dB over benchmarks (abstract). A search snippet says the OFDM literature "neglect[s] … time variations of the channel in high-speed scenarios" (paraphrase from the search summary; UNVERIFIED wording). — [arXiv:2404.07759](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07759)
- **Kaplan 2026 survey.** "mmWave/THz channel coherence times are on the order of 10–100 μs"; "optimization must be completed within a sub-millisecond timescale". Its GCDIM diffusion variant takes 70 ms, "two orders of magnitude" too slow. Static optimization "does not adapt to mobile environments without re-solving from scratch". The term "channel aging" does not appear (WebFetch, first 100k characters). — [arXiv:2606.31334](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31334)
- **Channel-aging-aware precoding for RIS-aided multi-user (narrowband, not OFDM), ~2022.** Projected gradient ascent; aging comes from Doppler and phase noise. Bibliographic data: UNVERIFIED (ResearchGate only). — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/364040212)

### Inferences
- The thesis's capstone fills a gap that this search could not close: subcarrier count as a compute/fidelity knob, evaluated at measured latency under channel aging. The closest conceptual precedents for an "optimal reduction level" argument are Yang et al.'s optimal grouping size (training versus flexibility) and An et al.'s coherence-time-matched training budget.
- The Kaplan survey's runtime claims (ML 10²–10⁴× faster) are self-reported literature values that exclude training. They are not comparable to the thesis's measured, idle-machine, single-thread latency protocol and should not be cited as a benchmark.

### Gaps
- No verified paper reports the iteration count or wall-clock time of a wideband RIS optimizer as a function of the number of subcarriers.
- IEEE Xplore full-text search was unavailable, so mobility-aware OFDM-RIS phase-tracking papers (e.g. Kalman/tracking-based) may have been missed. "Variational Bayesian Multiuser Tracking for RIS Aided MIMO-OFDM Systems" (Teng, Yuan, Wang et al., arXiv 2304.11884) is a tracking (estimation) paper and is relevant only as context. — [arXiv:2304.11884](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11884)
