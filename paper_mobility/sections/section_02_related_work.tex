\section{Related Work and Research Gaps}
\label{sec:related_work}

The design of phase shift matrices for Reconfigurable Intelligent Surfaces (RIS) has been extensively studied, yet existing literature operates primarily under static assumptions, ignoring algorithm execution latency. We categorize prior arts into four core domains to highlight our key contributions.

\subsection{Classical and Deep Learning-Based RIS Phase Optimization}
Classical phase optimization focuses on achieving mathematical convergence quality on static or quasi-static channels. Frameworks leveraging Alternating Optimization (AO) and Semidefinite Relaxation (SDR) \cite{Wu2019TWC}, Coordinate Descent (CD) \cite{Abeywickrama2020TCOM}, Majorization-Minimization (MM) \cite{Pan2020TSP}, and Fractional Programming (FP) \cite{Guo2020TCOM} achieve near-optimal beamforming bounds. However, their per-iteration complexity---ranging from $\mathcal{O}(K \cdot N^2)$ to $\mathcal{O}(N^{3.5})$---requires dozens of iterations ($100+$ steps), incurring processing delays ($T_{\text{proc}}$) of tens to hundreds of milliseconds.

To bypass this iterative burden, Deep Learning (DL) approaches shift computation offline. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) \cite{Elbir2020WCL, Feng2022TCOM}, Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) \cite{Huang2020TWC, Liu2021JSAC}, Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) \cite{Zhang2023TVT}, and Deep Unfolding Networks (DUNN) \cite{Song2022WCL} reduce online execution to sub-millisecond single-forward passes ($\mathcal{O}(N)$). Nevertheless, these DL models operate under the implicit assumption that "faster inference automatically yields higher physical performance," failing to benchmark model execution time ($T_{\text{proc}}$) against the physical channel coherence time ($T_{\text{coh}}$) under movement.

\subsection{Mobility, Channel Aging, and Predictive Beamforming}
In dynamic scenarios such as High-Speed Rail (HSR) \cite{Huang2021TWC} and Vehicular-to-Everything (V2X) \cite{Basar2019Access}, high Doppler shifts induce rapid channel aging \cite{Papazafeiropoulos2022TCOM, Ding2022TWC}. Phase matrices calculated based on instantaneous Channel State Information (CSI) at time $t$ become stale upon physical deployment at time $t + T_{\text{proc}}$.

To counter channel aging, predictive beamforming schemes using Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) \cite{Ma2020WCL}, trajectory tracking Kalman filters \cite{Liu2022TCOM}, and temporal Transformers \cite{Chen2024TVT, Zhao2023WCL} forecast future channels $\hat{\mathbf{H}}(t + T_{\text{proc}})$. However, \textbf{predictive beamforming fails to eliminate staleness because prediction itself consumes computation time}. The cumulative delay ($T_{\text{pred}} + T_{\text{opt}}$) still renders the phase configuration stale when applied to high-velocity propagation environments.

\subsection{Quantization and Hardware Constraints}
Practical hardware implementations deploy discrete phase shifts controlled via PIN diodes or varactors \cite{Abeywickrama2020TCOM}. Quantization-aware training and Straight-Through Estimator (STE) techniques \cite{Yang2021TCOM} demonstrate that 2-bit quantization (4 discrete levels) incurs minimal capacity loss ($\le 3\%$) under static CSI. However, prior works evaluate quantization noise in isolation, ignoring the compound degradation when 2-bit discrete phase shifts interact with outdated, stale channel states.

\subsection{Summary of Gaps and Our Novelty}
As summarized in Table~\ref{tab:lit_comparison}, prior arts exhibit fundamental research gaps:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Equal-Millisecond Misconception:} Existing works assume all algorithms share equal computational cost per step. We disprove this, showing a $2.7\times$ variation in per-iteration cost that completely reverses algorithm performance rankings under realistic time budgets.
    \item \textbf{Quantified Value of Freshness:} While delayed feedback has been studied in active MIMO \cite{Jindal2006IT}, no prior work has quantified the trade-off between fresh, partially converged phases versus stale, optimal phases for RIS. We prove that a fresh phase design is $11.7\text{--}14.2\times$ more valuable than a stale, mathematically optimal phase configuration.
    \item \textbf{Discovery of the Intrinsic Optimum ($L=4$):} Literature assumes deeper iteration counts ($L=48$) maximize effective rate. We prove that under mobility, mathematical convergence is \emph{not} physical rate optimal, establishing that truncating iterations at $L=4$ maximizes the physical Effective Rate ($R_A^{\text{eff}}$).
\end{enumerate}
